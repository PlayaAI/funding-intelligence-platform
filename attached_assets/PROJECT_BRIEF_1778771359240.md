# Project Brief: Proof Website + Grant Intelligence Operating System

## Working Name
Grant Intelligence Platform / Proof Package System / Playa AI Grant OS

The final name is undecided. Do not hardcode a final brand name unless instructed.

## One-Sentence Summary
Build a proof-driven public website plus a private Instrumentl-inspired grant intelligence dashboard that helps the team package its proof, find funders, prioritize top grants, collaborate on applications, and use AI-assisted workflows to prepare stronger submissions.

## Why This Exists
The team has already created meaningful work: workshops, apps, experiments, community tools, documents, and technologies. The problem is that the proof is scattered and the grant process is chaotic. A recent grant was nearly pursued but missed because the team started too late, had portal/access issues, and did not have a structured application workflow ready.

The platform exists to solve this operational problem:

- Stop last-minute grant scrambling.
- Make proof visible and reusable.
- Prioritize the best-fit grants instead of chasing every urgent deadline.
- Use project profiles to match grants and funders.
- Create a repeatable application workflow using Google Docs/Drive first.
- Build an internal system inspired by Instrumentl without copying it blindly.

## What We Are Building

### Layer 1: Public Proof Website
A public site for funders, grant reviewers, collaborators, partners, and community members.

Its job is to show:

- Who the organization/community is.
- What has already been built.
- What workshops happened.
- What tools and experiments exist.
- What proof and impact already exist.
- Why the team is credible and fundable.
- Which projects are grant-ready.

This website is **proof-driven**, not AI-driven.

### Layer 2: Internal Grant Intelligence Dashboard
A private dashboard for the team.

Its job is to support:

- Project profiles.
- Grant opportunity tracking.
- Opportunity matching.
- Funder intelligence.
- Peer organization research.
- Application workspaces.
- Task management.
- Document links.
- Proof package building.
- AI-assisted grant analysis, drafting, and proof matching.
- Agentic monitoring later.

## Core Product Question
The platform should help answer:

> Which grants should we apply for, why are we a strong fit, what proof do we have, and what needs to happen before submission?

Every feature should support that question.

## Meeting-Specific Context to Preserve

### Instrumentl Inspiration
The meeting was explicitly inspired by Instrumentl. Important concepts to preserve:

- Tracker view.
- Shortlisted grants.
- Grant detail overview.
- Funding opportunity documents.
- Task management.
- Document management.
- Opportunity matches.
- Funder matches.
- Peer organization/funder research.
- 990-style funding history.
- AI-assisted application writing.

### Project Matching
The team discussed adding projects into Instrumentl and receiving opportunity matches. Example project categories mentioned:

- Playa AI.
- Art, Science and Research.
- Decommodified Data Set.
- Democracy 2.0.
- Overall foundation.
- Tech for Human Flourishing.

The new platform must support project profiles as the foundation for grant matching.

### MIT Solve / Connect App
MIT Solve was discussed as a priority opportunity. The Connect App seemed like the strongest technology-centered candidate.

The Connect App is a guided human connection experience that helps people connect with someone they are meeting or already know. It prompts deeper interaction and can ask users to put the phone down and look into each other’s eyes. The team wanted to show more usage and session count as proof.

### Proof Package
The website and platform must help package proof. Proof includes:

- Physical events.
- Workshops.
- Documents.
- Best practices.
- Apps and technologies.
- Connect App.
- Ikigai App.
- Burning Man Packing List.
- Biohack Your Burn guide/site.
- Oracle Art Demo.
- Aaron Coombs relationship/contact support tool.
- Community experiments.

### Application Workflow
The team discussed using a shared Google Doc for each grant to collect application form questions, draft answers, get feedback, and collaborate before entering final answers into portals.

V1 should support Google Doc and Google Drive links. It should not build a full document editor.

### Top 3 Focus Grants
The team specifically discussed focusing on the most applicable grants instead of chasing everything due tomorrow. The dashboard should have a “Top 3 Focus Grants” section.

## Product Positioning
This is not a generic CRM and not a generic AI website.

Best positioning:

> A mission-control system for grant-funded projects: combining a public proof website, a reusable proof database, funder intelligence, peer organization research, grant tracking, application workspaces, and AI-assisted preparation.

## Primary Users

- Alex / grant strategy lead.
- Rebecca / grant research and document preparation.
- Raed / technical builder and automation/data workflow contributor.
- Contributors who submit projects and proof.
- Advisors/reviewers who give feedback.
- Future users from related nonprofit/art/community projects.

## Primary Outcomes

1. A grant reviewer can understand the team’s credibility from the public site.
2. The team can see top grant priorities at a glance.
3. Each project has a reusable profile for matching and applications.
4. Each grant has a workspace with deadline, requirements, Google Doc, tasks, and proof.
5. The team can research peer organizations and discover funders.
6. AI helps summarize, score, draft, and suggest proof, but humans approve all final work.

## What Not To Build First

- Full Instrumentl clone.
- Full Google Docs editor.
- Full Google Drive API integration.
- Full 990 parser.
- Complex scraping system.
- CRM for donor management.
- Post-award grant reporting.
- Auto-submission to grant portals.
- Auto-emailing funders.
